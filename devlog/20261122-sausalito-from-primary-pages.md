## Corrections to the first reading

- **Measure X** failed 57.9 percent to 42.1 (2,471 to 1,798) on the county's count of
  20 November; the result is certified on 25 November [primary]. Its name is the Arts,
  Industrial and Maritime Preservation and Improvement Initiative.
- **The council race** (two seats): Hoffman 1,984, Saad 1,773, Brinton 1,535, Cox 1,432, on
  the same count [primary]. An incumbent is in last place.
- **The budget** is $29,396,600: $24,379,848 of general fund operations and $5,016,752 of
  capital projects, balanced with $1,135,452 of unassigned balance and $462,872 from a
  pension trust [primary: staff report, 16 June 2026].
- **The anchorage** programme ended on 15 October 2026 by the regional agency's own report,
  not 26 October. 52 people were housed since 2022 and 16.5 acres of eelgrass restored. Four
  vessels remain on the bay, legally [primary: board packet, 12 November 2026].
- **Galilee Harbor** is counted as city housing in the adopted housing plan (38 berths), so it
  is inside the city on that evidence.
- **The ferry**: the Sausalito route alone carried 377,595 riders in the year to mid-2025,
  against 629,954 in 2019 [primary].
- **Sewers**: the sanitary district says it took over the town's sewer pipes on 22 August
  2025 [primary]. A city notice is reported to give 26 September.
- **Evacuation zones**: the county's layer has 14 zones for Sausalito, not nine.
- **County parcels and footprints** carry an "as is" disclaimer and no open licence. The
  footprints have no heights.

## How the town is run

A general-law city since 1893 with a council and a manager. Five members serve overlapping
four-year terms, and "the Council elects one councilmember to serve as mayor and one ... as
vice mayor" [primary]. For 2026: Mayor Steven Woodside, Vice Mayor Melissa Blaustein, Joan
Cox, Jill James Hoffman, Ian Patrick Sobieski. The choice each December is known only from a
summary; no December 2026 agenda was posted when this was read. An interim city manager is in
post from 1 September 2026 to 28 February 2027.

Departments: Administration (manager's office, clerk, finance, human resources, legal, IT),
Community Development (planning, building, the General Plan), Public Works (streets, stairs,
storm drains, waterfront infrastructure, capital projects, floodplain administration), Police
(with parking and a marine patrol), Parks and Recreation, the Library. Fire is the Southern
Marin Fire District, a separate agency.

Roles that would have a use for a playable model, as published in the staff directory: the
Resiliency and Sustainability Manager, the Community Development Director and planners, the
Public Works Director and City Engineer and the project manager for the Gate 5 study, the
Emergency Services Coordinator, the Parking Programs Manager, and a communications
coordinator. The directory lists no GIS post and no IT post; an IT manager was funded for
2026-27.

Bodies of residents: the Planning Commission, the Sustainability Commission ("climate
resilience, equity, and environmental" matters), Historic Preservation, Parks and Recreation,
Pedestrian and Bicycle Advisory, Economic Development Advisory, Community Safety and Disaster
Preparedness, the Library Board, and a Sea Level Rise Task Force formed in 2021 whose current
status is unclear.

## Rising water

The draft Shoreline Adaptation Plan (168 pages, dated 12 November 2025) is still a draft.
On 18 November 2025 the council acknowledged the threat and asked for engineered ways to
protect the present shoreline, financing options, cost estimates and work with waterfront
businesses. Nothing further is posted for 2026 [primary].

The plan's water levels, with a typical high tide at 5.9 ft:

| Scenario | Total water level (NAVD88) | Cost of doing nothing |
|---|---|---|
| 2050, sea up 0.8 ft | 6.7 ft | $160 to 570 million |
| 2050, plus a 100-year surge of 3.5 ft | 10.2 ft | $500 million to $1.8 billion |
| 2100, sea up 3.1 ft with surge, or up 6.6 ft | 12.5 ft | $1.2 to 4.2 billion |

In the third: 110 storm drains, 24,400 ft of sewer pipe, 49,500 ft of road and 5,000 ft of
evacuation route. The city's FAQ gives a different range for 2100 (2.4 to 6.9 ft), and says
the main sewer runs under Bridgeway.

**Gate 5** is where it is happening now. A drainage study (a federal grant of $614,400;
survey in early 2026) reported to the council on 1 September 2026. The council then took
$250,000 from its emergency reserve for flood works there, ratified a local emergency for the
El Niño winter on 20 October, and on 17 November let a contract for a tide gate on the Coloma
Canal (up to $285,000). Earlier studies of flooding (2002) and of the ground sinking (2009,
covering 1968 to 2007, and 2011) are linked from the city's page and were not opened.

## Housing

The plan adopted on 27 May 2025 has to make room for 724 homes: 200 very low income, 115
low, 114 moderate, 295 above moderate. Its own tables give the capacity found as 923, 925 or
935 depending on the page. About 352 come from projects already approved, small sites and
second units, and 571 from rezoned opportunity sites. In November 2026 the city named two
finalist teams to build on the MLK property and the Corporation Yard [primary].

## The Marinship

By the City Attorney's analysis [primary], Measure X would have repealed the 1989 specific
plan, let the council rezone without a vote outside the historic downtown, and raised what
may be built there: lot coverage from 30 or 50 percent to 70, floor area from 0.3 or 0.4 times
the lot to 3.0, and height from 32 to 47 ft. Three campaigns formed: Sausalitans for a
Thriving Waterfront (for), Save Our Sausalito (against), and the Sausalito Working Waterfront
Coalition (against). A local paper reported a 21 percent office vacancy and more than $100
million of deferred repairs [news: Pacific Sun, 13 October 2026]. On 1 September 2026 the
council considered a financing district for the Marinship's infrastructure. The city has no
"hot topic" page for the Marinship.

## What the council dealt with, September to November 2026

From the agendas [primary]: the Gate 5 study and emergency flood works; king tide and El
Niño preparation; the financing district for the Marinship; the condition of the Turney
Street dock; an appeal of a housing project under state law; restriping Caledonia Street;
grant support for Bridgeway and for Gate 6 Road to Harbor Drive; a tide gate; a report on
realigning Willow Creek; rules for second units; downtown wayfinding; holiday parking.
