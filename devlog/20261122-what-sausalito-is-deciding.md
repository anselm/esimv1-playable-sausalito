# What Sausalito is deciding

22 November 2026. A first reading of the town, gathered in one sitting by a research
agent (Claude Opus, web reading only) and written up by Claude (Fable 5.2). It is a list
of leads for choosing what the model should address first. It is not a checked record.

## How far to trust this page

Each claim carries one of these marks:

- **[primary]**: read on the agency's own page or data service.
- **[news]**: read in a news report.
- **[summary]**: taken from a search engine's summary because the page would not load.
  The city's own site (sausalito.gov) returned only its menus, and Marin County's pages
  refused the connection, so most city and county facts carry this mark.
- **[transcript]**: from sausalito-cc.pages.dev, which posts machine transcripts of
  council meetings and does not say who runs it.

Nothing here was checked with anyone in the town. Every row needs a primary page before
the model or a public page leans on it.

## The town

- 7,269 people at the 2020 census on 1.76 square miles of land [summary]. The state's
  estimate for 1 January 2026 is 6,878 people and 4,474 housing units [primary:
  Department of Finance table E-1, May 2026].
- A general-law city with five council members. The council chooses a mayor and a vice
  mayor from its members each December for one year [news: KALW, 1 October 2026;
  summary]. The mayor for 2026 is Steven Woodside [summary]. Two seats were on the
  ballot on 3 November 2026 and the count was not certified when this was read.
- A city manager runs the staff. The post changed hands twice in 2026 and an interim
  manager was appointed on 3 September for six months [news].
- The budget adopted in June 2026 is reported as $29.4 million, of which $24.4 million
  is general fund operations, with about $1.1 million drawn from reserves [transcript].
  A local outlet gave $23.8 million for operations a month earlier. Revenue comes from
  property tax, sales tax (a 1 percent local rate since a 2022 measure), hotel tax and
  parking. No ranked breakdown was found.

## The issues

**Housing.** The state requires the town to plan for 724 new homes between 2023 and
2031, against 79 in the previous period [summary]. Sausalito's own law requires a vote
of residents to rezone. In November 2025 voters approved rezoning twelve commercial
sites (Measure J, about 75 percent) and, narrowly, up to 50 homes on part of the MLK
Park property (Measure K, 54 percent) [summary]. A housing group's lawsuit over the plan
was dismissed in February 2026 and is on appeal [advocacy group's own page].

**The Marinship.** About 200 acres of working waterfront on wartime fill, governed by a
1989 specific plan that keeps it industrial and maritime. A citizens' measure on
3 November 2026 (Measure X) would have repealed that plan and moved zoning decisions
from voters to the council; early counts had it failing, 58 percent against [news: KQED,
13 November 2026, not certified]. The ground is reported to be sinking; the figure of up
to 4 feet by 2100 is attributed to Scripps in a television report and the study itself
was not found.

**Sea level rise.** A draft Shoreline Adaptation Plan was returned as incomplete in
November 2025, and in January 2026 the council asked for costs, engineering and a way to
finance the work [news; transcript]. Gate 5 Road and Gate 6 Road flood at high tides now
[summary: county transport study, May 2025]. Published projections for 2100 disagree:
2.4 to 6.9 feet on the city's own page, 37 inches in a city news item, 3.5 to 12 feet in
a television report. The disagreement is itself something a model should show. The main
sewer is said to run under Bridgeway, which floods permanently in the county study at 49
inches of rise [summary].

**The anchorage.** In 2016 more than 240 boats were anchored on Richardson Bay without
permission, many of them homes. Under an agreement with the bay commission the regional
agency removed them to protect eelgrass, with a final deadline of 26 October 2026; 51
people were housed through its programs and 14.5 acres of eelgrass were replanted
[primary: the agency's newsletter and release]. Sausalito is not a member of that agency
and manages its own waters. No report from after the deadline was found.

**Floating homes.** The large houseboat harbors north of town (Waldo Point, 245 to 282
homes depending on the count) are in the unincorporated county, outside city limits
[summary]. Galilee Harbor appears to be inside; not confirmed.

**Visitors, the ferry, bicycles and parking.** The ferry system carried 1.58 million
riders on all routes in the year to mid-2025 [summary]; no Sausalito-only figure was
found. Day visitors on rented bicycles and parking downtown and on Caledonia Street are
standing subjects at the council [transcript]. No traffic counts for Bridgeway were
found.

**The hillside.** A slide on 14 February 2019 came off park land above Sausalito
Boulevard; the US Geological Survey published a lidar survey of it [summary]. Caltrans
was clearing a rock slide on Highway 101 south of town in September 2026 [primary]. A
hillside task force was raised at the council in January 2026 [transcript].

**Sewers.** The sanitary district took over all sewer service in the town in August
2025 [summary].

**Wildfire and evacuation.** The town is divided into nine evacuation zones [summary].
Nothing was read on fuel, insurance or how long the hillside streets take to empty.

**Marin City.** The neighbouring community is unincorporated, shares a school district
with Sausalito (under a state desegregation order since 2019) and floods at its one
road entrance, where the county runs a pump and plans a permanent station [summary].

**Homelessness.** The county counted 1,128 people in January 2026 [summary]. No figure
for the town was found.

## Who holds which lever

| Body | What it decides |
|---|---|
| The council | The budget, parking, the shoreline plan, and zoning subject to a vote |
| Residents, by ballot | Zoning changes |
| The state housing department and the courts | Whether the housing plan complies |
| The bay commission (BCDC) | Fill and building on the shoreline; the anchorage agreement |
| The Richardson's Bay Regional Agency | The anchorage outside the town's waters |
| Marin County | The floating-home harbors, Marin City, flood works, homelessness programs |
| Caltrans | Highway 101 |
| The National Park Service | The land above the town |
| The Golden Gate Bridge district | The ferry |
| The Sausalito-Marin City Sanitary District | Sewers |
| The Southern Marin Fire Protection District | Fire and evacuation |

Who supplies water was not confirmed.

## Data a model could stand on

- **Elevation.** One-metre elevation models from US Geological Survey lidar cover the
  area (a 2018 survey and one published in August 2024), public domain [primary:
  queried from the National Map service].
- **Tides.** The gauge at the Golden Gate (NOAA station 9414290, records from 1854) is
  the nearest that is running. Two gauges at Sausalito itself ran only from 1977 to 1979
  [primary].
- **Flood layers.** The US Geological Survey's coastal storm model (CoSMoS, shown in
  Our Coast Our Future), the bay commission's Adapting to Rising Tides explorer, and the
  county's BayWAVE study. Licences vary by release and were not all read.
- **Parcels, zoning and building footprints.** Marin County's map services. A
  copyright notice is shown and no open licence is stated; this has to be asked.
- **People.** The census and the state's yearly estimates.

## What already exists

The town and county already have flood maps a resident can open: the city's own sea
level rise map, the county's viewer and a Richardson Bay story map, and the two regional
explorers above. They show where water would stand at a chosen rise. None of those found
lets a person choose a response and see what follows from it, and nothing called a
digital twin was found.
