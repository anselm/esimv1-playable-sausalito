Ground: US Geological Survey 3DEP one-metre lidar elevation, 2018 survey, drawn at 4 m. Trees: whatever stands 3 m or more above the ground outside buildings in the same survey's points (US Geological Survey), one body per 7 m of canopy. Creeks are OpenStreetMap's, and the fainter drainage lines under "layers" are computed here from the lidar ground. Roads are OpenStreetMap's lines drawn as geometry on the ground, as wide as their lanes on the map or as their class usually is; bridges stand at the height the lidar saw. Buildings, roads and piers: © OpenStreetMap contributors. Building heights: . Clock time is Pacific Standard Time. Water: a flat surface at the level of NOAA's San Francisco tide gauge (9414290), measured or predicted. Ground below that level shows wet whether or not the bay can reach it; walls, drains and pumps are not in the picture. The waves are decoration scaled by the measured wind. Piers are drawn as if all of them float. Sky: the nearest National Weather Service observation. Boats: each patch of 2018 lidar returns standing over the water, away from buildings and piers, is drawn as one hull, as long and wide as the patch and lying the way it lies, with a mast as tall as its tallest return or a cabin where it was low. Boats that touch count as one, and which end is the bow is a guess. Floating homes are OpenStreetMap's outlines; where the lidar gave no height theirs is a placeholder. Small round marks: places named in the news the inhaler has read.
